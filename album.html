THE NIGHT
SHIFT

Seven Detroit-length tracks, one night on the clock — about 16 and a half minutes, built to run with no skips and loop twice on a cross-town drive. Every song was cut in the same 21-day July sprint, on his own beats, on one sound: keys and violins over a pitched 808, triple-time hats, and one bare moment per record where the noise drops and he's alone. Clock-in, the block, the crew, the doubt at 3 AM, the pay-off, the mirror at dawn.

WHY

THE SHAPE

one summer · one sound · one night

The material already is the form. The three finished anchors run 2:22, 2:24 and 2:49 — Detroit length. Two sit in D♯ minor, one in G minor next to Equity. The tape opens and closes in the home key.

The numbers are a thread. He released 6738 in 2022. This tape carries 6734 — four doors down, four years later. The catalog is quietly a map, and the sequence reads it out loud.

The sprint was all his. Every anchor and every first-choice pick was cut on his own production — the vault shows the borrowed-beat sessions all live outside the July window. This tape is the month the sound became self-contained.

Where he is: six years in, five releases already this year, mixes maturing on a measured line every drop. Not an introduction — a consolidation.
